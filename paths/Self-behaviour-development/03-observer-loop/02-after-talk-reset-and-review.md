# After a difficult conversation — review without shame

## What this is

After a hard conversation or a difficult exchange, the mind tends to run a prosecutorial replay: everything that went wrong, everything you said badly, evidence that you ruined it. This loop is not useful. It does not produce insight — it produces shame and fatigue.

This file replaces that loop with a three-line write-up that takes under two minutes and actually helps.

## When to use this

- After any conversation that felt difficult, tense, or like it went sideways
- When you notice yourself replaying an exchange repeatedly
- At the end of a hard day, before bed, to close things down instead of leaving them open
- Any time the "I ruined everything" story starts running

## How to do it

As soon as possible after a difficult exchange, write three lines. No more.

| Line | What to write |
|------|---------------|
| What happened | One neutral sentence describing the event. No interpretation. Just what occurred: "I received a message accusing me of X. I replied by Y." |
| How I responded | Two or three descriptive words only — no judgment. Not "I was terrible". Just: "short", "loud", "defensive", "cold". |
| One thing I would do differently | Name one specific behaviour for next time. Not a character resolution ("I will be calmer"). A specific action: "I would wait 20 minutes before responding." |

That is it. No fourth line. No retrospective analysis. The "everything is ruined" version of events is not allowed in this table.

**After writing:**

Take one slow breath out. Drop your shoulders. Say to yourself: "that was one exchange, not a verdict". Then close the notebook or the document.

**Evening check:**

If shame starts up at bedtime, pause it with the table. Dictate or handwrite the three lines if typing feels like too much. The structure matters — it forces specificity and stops the spiral.

## Example

Difficult conversation: a tense call that ended badly.

- What happened: "The call became an argument about the schedule. I raised my voice near the end and it finished abruptly."
- How I responded: "Rushed, loud, cut off."
- One thing differently: "I would have said 'let me call you back tomorrow' instead of continuing when I was already too activated."

Done. That is the whole review.
