# Noticing your body, your impulse, and the story — separately

## What this is

In a difficult moment, three things happen at once: your body reacts (tension, heat, held breath), you feel an impulse (to flee, to attack, to go silent), and your mind starts telling a story about what this means. Usually all three collapse into one automatic reaction.

This skill is about learning to notice the three channels separately, rather than fusing into the first story that arrives. You do not need to act on any of them immediately. You just need to see them as distinct.

This matters because the story your mind offers first — especially under stress — is often wrong. Separating the channels gives you a moment to choose, rather than react.

## When to use this

- During a difficult conversation, to stay steady instead of being swept along
- After a difficult exchange, to understand what actually happened
- When you feel a strong automatic reaction and want to understand where it is coming from
- As a daily practice to build the habit of observation

## How to do it

**During a difficult moment:**

As something is happening, notice which channel is loudest. Just name it internally — body, impulse, or story. You do not need to do anything else. Just the naming creates a small gap.

**Practice (15 prompts):**

Work through a list of 15 recent situations or imagined scenarios — anything that would normally activate you. For each one, identify which channel came first:
- **Body** — physical sensation (tightness, heat, held breath)
- **Impulse** — what you wanted to do (leave, fight, go silent, scroll away)
- **Story** — what your mind said it meant ("they always do this", "this proves they hate me")

Answer quickly. No need to defend or analyse your answer.

**Two-minute sit:**

Sit quietly for two minutes. Every time you notice a craving or impulse (check the phone, replay a scenario, imagine a response), say "impulse" internally, then take one breath. You are not trying to eliminate impulses. You are practising catching them before they become actions.

**After a difficult conversation:**

Write down three tokens — three words that describe what happened internally:
- Were you in observer mode (noticing without reacting)?
- What was the impulse?
- Where did you slip and on what?

## The right order

Observe what is happening in your body and mind first. Then calm your body if needed. Then check whether your interpretation is accurate. Then choose what to say. Never reverse this order when you are activated.
