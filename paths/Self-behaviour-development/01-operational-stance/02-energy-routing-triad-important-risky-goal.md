# Does this actually matter? The three-question filter

## What this is

Before you react to something — a message, a comment, a dig — run it through three quick questions. This takes under five seconds and stops you from spending real energy on things that do not deserve it.

The three questions are:
1. **Does this matter to my actual responsibilities?**
2. **Is there real risk here — something with consequences?**
3. **Is there a real goal I can actually work toward?**

If the answer to all three is no — stay quiet or respond minimally. Not every provocation needs a response. Not every injustice needs your energy right now.

## When to use this

- You receive a message and feel the pull to respond immediately
- Someone says something designed to get a reaction from you
- You notice yourself about to enter an argument that has no real endpoint
- You are unsure whether to engage or let something go

## How to do it

**The filter (under 5 seconds):**

Ask the three questions mentally. Use simple yes/no answers:
- Important = yes / no
- Risk = yes / no
- Real goal = yes / no / none

If importance and goal both come back no — the answer is minimal response or silence.

If there is risk but no workable goal (you cannot resolve this through direct conversation) — shift to a factual, low-temperature approach, or handle it through the appropriate channel (written record, professional escalation) rather than a live argument.

**Weekly practice:**

Once a week, identify one place where you are regularly giving energy that returns nothing — a person, a group chat, a feed. Write down one clear limit for it. Note once if you slipped, without judgment.

## Example

Someone sends a message taking a dig at a past decision you made. You feel the urge to defend yourself.

Filter check:
- Does this matter to my responsibilities? No.
- Is there real risk? No.
- Is there a real goal? No — this person is not going to change their view and nothing material depends on this exchange.

Response: minimal or none.
