# Short phrases to stay grounded

## What this is

A set of short memorised phrases you say to yourself — not to anyone else — that help you stay oriented when things get hard. They work faster than trying to reason your way through a difficult moment, because they bypass the mental argument and land directly in your body.

There are three layers, each for a different type of day:

- **Daily phrases** — short, used every morning or whenever you need a reset
- **Weekly phrases** — slightly wider, used a few times a week to connect your actions to what matters
- **Deep phrases** — for genuinely hard days, not for ordinary frustration

The layers are not a hierarchy. You do not graduate to the deep ones. You match the phrase to the day.

## When to use this

- First thing in the morning before anything begins
- Before a difficult conversation or meeting
- When you feel yourself drifting — flat, reactive, or checked out
- On a genuinely hard day when you feel lost or numb

## How to do it

**Write your own phrases.** The ones below are prompts to help you build yours. They should feel like things you actually believe, not borrowed language that sounds right but lands hollow.

**Daily layer (30 seconds, every morning):**
Take a slow breath in and a longer breath out. Say your short phrases quietly — between breaths, not over them. Do not journal immediately after. Just let it sit.

Example prompt: What do I actually care about today? What is one true thing about my situation?

**Weekly layer (a few minutes, a few times a week):**
Say your wider phrases slowly, out loud. Then name one specific behaviour you are going to lead with that day — something concrete. ("I will not draft that message before I have slept on it." / "I will move my body before I open anything difficult.")

At the end of the day, ask yourself one yes/no question: did I do that one thing? No essay required.

**Deep layer (for genuinely hard days):**
When you feel numb, lost, or like nothing is moving — say your deep phrases once, quietly. Then do one small physical thing in the next ten minutes. Eat something. Walk one block. Open one specific file you have been avoiding. The phrase alone does nothing. The phrase plus one small action breaks the inertia.

## One important limit

These phrases are for keeping yourself grounded, not for getting yourself worked up for a confrontation. If you find yourself using them to prepare for an argument, that is a signal to step back, not forward.
