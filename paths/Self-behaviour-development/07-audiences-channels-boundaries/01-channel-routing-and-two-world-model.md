# What you say live vs what goes in writing — keep them separate

## What this is

There are two different worlds for what you communicate, and they have different rules.

**Live conversation** — phone calls, in-person exchanges, verbal responses in meetings or hearings. These are judged on tone, composure, and brevity. Keep them short, factual, and calm. Do not try to argue a complex case from memory in a live setting.

**Written and official channels** — emails, formal statements, legal documents, anything on the record. These can be reviewed, edited, and are permanent. Everything here needs to be precise, considered, and checked before it goes out. Nothing is improvised.

The danger is mixing them up. The most common mistake is trying to rebuild a complex argument live — from memory, under stress — when that argument belongs in writing with proper review. The improvised version is almost always worse than the written version, and it cannot be taken back.

## When to use this

- You are about to respond to something legally or formally significant in a live conversation
- You feel the urge to "just explain everything" verbally when the topic is serious
- You are deciding whether to respond now (live/verbal) or wait and put it in writing
- Something sensitive comes up in a conversation and you are not sure what to say

## How to do it

**Before you speak, ask:** does this belong in writing?

Things that belong in the live channel:
- Brief, factual statements about your own observable behaviour
- Simple logistical responses you know with certainty
- Calm, composed tone — no drama, no lengthy arguments

Things that belong in writing or with an advisor:
- Anything involving legal details or formal positions
- Complex explanations of past events
- Anything you are not certain about
- Anything where the exact wording matters

**When something sensitive comes up live that belongs in writing:**
You do not have to answer it on the spot. Say: "That's something I want to address properly — I'll put it in writing." Then stop.

**Never try to reconstruct a formal argument from memory under pressure.** If you are not sure, say you will follow up in writing. That is always the safer choice.

## Example

You are in a meeting and someone asks: "Can you explain exactly what happened in the incident on [date] and why you responded the way you did?"

This is not a live-conversation answer. The details matter too much to improvise.

Live response: "That's something I want to address accurately. I'll put a clear account in writing."
