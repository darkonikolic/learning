# When engaging makes things worse — learning to stop feeding the loop

## What this is

Some conversations, people, and channels get worse the more you engage with them. Every response you add becomes more material. Every explanation invites another challenge. Every clarification gets used as a new angle of attack.

Anxiety pushes you to engage. It feels like if you just explain one more thing, clarify one more misunderstanding, or correct one more false claim, things will get better. They usually do not. Anxiety mistakes verbosity for control.

The discipline here is recognising the difference between two things:
- Responding because it is genuinely necessary
- Responding because the silence is uncomfortable and you cannot sit with it

The second one is where the damage happens.

## When to use this

- You have already said what needed to be said and feel the urge to add more
- You are in a thread or exchange that gets more hostile the more you engage
- You just hit send and are now composing the follow-up message
- Someone is not responding and you are about to fill the silence with more words
- You notice yourself replying in long messages to short provocations

## How to do it

**Step 1 — Name the pattern.**
Look at five things you have said or written in the past that were driven by anxiety rather than necessity. What did they add? In most cases, they added length but not substance, and they gave the other person more to respond to.

**Step 2 — Write one replacement line for each.**
For each over-explanation, find one sentence that covers the essential point and nothing more. Practice saying it out loud.

**Step 3 — Sit with the discomfort.**
When the urge to explain yourself hits, do not respond for at least two minutes. Notice the urge. It will peak and then soften. You do not need to act on every urge to explain.

**Step 4 — Check your CC lists and forward habits.**
Adding more people to a thread, forwarding messages to create a record, or cc-ing others as a form of pressure — these usually escalate things. Ask before you add anyone: does this serve the outcome, or does it serve my anxiety?

**Step 5 — After answering, stop.**
No postscripts. No "just one more thing." Once you have said what needed to be said, close the conversation and leave it.

## Example

You send a message. You do not get a response for two days. You feel the urge to send a follow-up: "Just checking in to make sure you got this — I want to be clear that I wasn't trying to imply..."

Stop. The follow-up is being driven by anxiety about how the first message landed, not by any genuine need. Do not send it.
