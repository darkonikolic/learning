# When you are frozen — separating real threats from fear stories

## What this is

When you are stuck, overwhelmed, or unable to move forward on something, there are two very different things that might be happening. The first is a learned fear — your mind is running a catastrophic story based on older experiences, not on what is actually in front of you right now. The second is a real material threat — something that actually needs action, possibly professional help.

These feel identical from the inside. Treating a learned fear story like an urgent real threat keeps you spinning in circles. Treating a real threat like just anxiety causes you to avoid getting actual help.

## When to use this

- When you have been stuck on the same problem or thought for more than 30 minutes
- When a fear feels urgent but you cannot identify the actual next step
- When you are exhausted by your own internal arguments about a problem
- During any period where you feel blocked multiple days in a row

## How to do it

**Step 1 — List what is worrying you.**
Write down up to ten things that are causing anxiety right now. Do not filter or rank them yet.

**Step 2 — Separate each one.**
Mark each item with either L (learned fear — a story your mind tells, based on older patterns, not a current material fact) or R (real threat — something that actually requires a concrete action or professional help).

**Step 3 — Deal with each correctly.**
- R items: do not argue with yourself about them. Get actual help. A lawyer, a doctor, a professional, a trusted person. Solo internal debate does not resolve real threats.
- L items: do not feed them with more analysis. Go to physical regulation first — movement, breathing, grounding. Then come back to thinking.

**Step 4 — If you have been stuck on a task for more than 30 minutes:**
Set a timer for five minutes. Do only actual work on the task for those five minutes. When the timer ends, stop and log whether you worked or stopped. That is it. No longer sessions required.

**Step 5 — During a blocked period (multiple days in a row):**
Pick one small forward step each day and do it. One thing. That is enough.

## Example

You have been going around in circles about a legal situation for two hours. You label it: "Is this an L or an R?" The threat is real — it needs a lawyer, not more internal debate. You stop arguing with yourself. You write "call lawyer" as tomorrow's one action and close the loop for tonight.
