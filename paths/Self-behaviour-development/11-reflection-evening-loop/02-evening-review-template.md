# Evening review template — 90 seconds, three questions

> Run after dinner, not immediately after the hardest moment of the day. Distance is part of the tool.

---

## The three questions

Answer each in one sentence. If you run over, restart shorter. Brevity is the gate — longer means you're storytelling, not auditing.

**Q1: What fact changed objectively today?**

Not feeling. Not interpretation. What actually happened that is verifiable?

> Example: "The court date was postponed to July." / "I sent the document." / "The conversation happened and lasted 20 minutes."

Answer: ________

---

**Q2: What did I do today that was oriented toward stability — not toward proving something to someone?**

Acts count. Thoughts and intentions do not.

> Example: "I slept before midnight." / "I walked instead of responding to that message." / "I stayed on topic during the call."

Answer: ________

---

**Q3: Which audience was I performing toward today — and can they actually supply the validation I was seeking?**

Name the audience honestly. Then ask the hard question.

> Example audience: "My lawyer." "My ex-partner." "Some imagined panel of judges." "My own internal critic."
> 
> Can they supply what I was seeking? Often: no. The partner cannot validate me. The lawyer evaluates procedure, not character. The panel doesn't exist.

Audience: ________  
Can they supply it: Yes / No / Partly

---

## Warning signs in the answers

These patterns signal the review is being used for rumination, not audit:

- Q1 answer includes emotional language: "it was unfair that..."
- Q2 answer is about what you avoided feeling, not what you did
- Q3 answer names the same audience 5+ days in a row with no shift
- Review takes more than 5 minutes

If any of these appear — stop, reset the sequence, answer shorter.
