# Pause, say less, stop — the core communication habit

## What this is

When you are under pressure, the instinct is to keep talking — to fill the silence, add more context, explain one more thing. This habit works against you. It makes you harder to listen to, easier to interrupt, and more likely to say something you did not mean to say.

The habit to build instead is simple: pause before you speak, answer in short chunks of one to three sentences, then stop and wait. Silence after you finish is not a problem. It is not a signal that you said something wrong. It is just silence.

## When to use this

- In any formal setting: a meeting, a hearing, an evaluation, a difficult conversation
- When someone asks you a question and you feel the urge to give a long answer
- When anxiety is making you want to fill every quiet moment with words
- When you notice yourself saying "...and another thing"

## How to do it

**Step 1 — Before you answer:**
Take at least two seconds before speaking. Do not fill that gap with filler sounds. Just pause. Use the time to decide what you actually want to say.

**Step 2 — Answer in chunks:**
Give your answer in one to three sentences. Then stop. Do not add more. Do not qualify everything. Do not explain what you just said. Say it, then close your mouth.

**Step 3 — Sit with the silence:**
After you stop talking, wait. The other person will respond or ask a follow-up question. You do not need to rescue the moment. Remind yourself: silence is not failure, it is just a pause.

**Step 4 — Kill "...and another thing":**
This phrase is the enemy. Every time you feel it coming, catch it and stop. If something is important, it can be its own answer when asked, not a tail you attach to the previous one.

## Example

Someone asks: "Why didn't you respond to that message earlier this week?"

Bad version: "Well I was dealing with a lot and I wasn't sure what to say and honestly I didn't want to make things worse and I thought maybe if I waited things would settle down and also I wasn't sure if it was even my place to respond and..."

Good version: "I needed some time to think before responding." Then stop.
