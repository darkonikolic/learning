# Inherited narrative audit — what is mine, what was installed

> Not all beliefs you carry about yourself are yours. Some were installed — through repetition, pressure, or long-term dynamics in which someone else defined who you are. This audit separates them.

---

## Why this is necessary

When someone tells you who you are often enough and loudly enough — you start to believe them. Especially if that person was close, if you were exhausted, or if you were in a position where defending yourself consumed all available energy.

The result: you carry a story about yourself that isn't yours. You act from it, make decisions from it, sometimes defend it even when it harms you.

The goal of this audit is not to convince you that you are perfect. The goal is to give you freedom through **distinction** — what is mine, what is not — so you can choose what you keep.

---

## Part 1 — Belief inventory

Write your answers to these questions. You're writing for yourself, not for an audience. There are no correct answers.

**What do I believe about myself in these areas:**

```
As a parent:              ________
As a partner:             ________
As a person in conflict:  ________
As someone who makes mistakes: ________
My worth:                 ________
My stability:             ________
My capability:            ________
```

Do not filter. Write what you actually believe — even if it embarrasses you.

---

## Part 2 — Origin test

For each belief you wrote, ask these three questions:

**Question A: Where does this belief come from?**

```
[ ] My own experience — I see this in my behaviour over time
[ ] Someone else's assessment — someone told me this, repeatedly or forcefully
[ ] Combination — partly mine, partly absorbed
[ ] I don't know
```

**Question B: When did I start believing this?**

```
Before a specific person / situation: ________
During or after: ________
```

If you held this belief before — it's likely yours or older, not installed by that person.
If it appeared during or after a specific dynamic — that is a signal.

**Question C: Who benefits from me believing this?**

```
Me (it makes me better, holds me accountable): ________
Someone else (it weakens my position, justifies their actions): ________
```

This is not a conspiracy theory. This is a practical question: does this belief work for you or against you?

---

## Part 3 — Most commonly installed beliefs (recognise if any are yours)

These are beliefs frequently installed through prolonged conflictual dynamics or toxic relationships. Read the list. Mark those you recognise in yourself.

```
[ ] "I am too emotional / unstable."
[ ] "I can't trust my own read of situations."
[ ] "I'm always the one who ruins things."
[ ] "My needs are too much."
[ ] "The children would be better off without my influence."
[ ] "I'm not good enough as a parent / partner / person."
[ ] "Others see clearly — I'm the biased party."
[ ] "My anger / sadness / fear proves I'm in the wrong."
[ ] "When I'm happy, it's a brief pause — I'm fundamentally a bad person."
[ ] "I have to try twice as hard to prove I'm normal."
```

For each one marked: apply the test from Part 2.

---

## Part 4 — Separation

For beliefs that are **installed** (not yours), write:

```
Belief: ________

What is actually true in this (if anything): ________

What is exaggerated, distorted, or untrue: ________

What would I say about this if I were rested, safe, and free of pressure? ________
```

That last answer — "what I would say without pressure" — is the starting point for your own narrative.

---

## Part 5 — Operational rule

When you receive a message, comment, or legal document that activates one of those installed beliefs:

1. Recognise: "this is targeting an installed story about me"
2. Do not defend against the installed belief — defending it confirms it
3. Return to your own narrative (file 03 of this phase)
4. If a response is needed — respond from your story, not theirs
