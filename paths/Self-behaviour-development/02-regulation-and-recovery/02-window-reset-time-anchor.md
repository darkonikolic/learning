# Recognising when you are off — and how to reset

## What this is

Sometimes you are not just stressed — you are outside your functional state entirely. Looping thoughts, body locked up, pulled toward an argument you cannot stop thinking about, unable to think in a straight line. This is different from ordinary stress.

This file helps you recognise the signs that you are in that state, gives you a reset sequence that takes under 30 seconds, and teaches you how to buy honest time when you need it — without lying about why.

## When to use this

- Your thoughts are cycling through the same conflict over and over
- Your voice or neck feels locked up
- You feel a magnetic pull toward the argument — you keep coming back to it
- You cannot think clearly about anything else
- You are about to respond to something and you know you should not yet

## How to do it

**Step 1 — Know your own signs.**

Write down six things that happen to you specifically when you are in this state. Not generic signs — yours. Examples: jaw clenches, words come out sharper, you start skim-reading, you open the same message five times. Post this list somewhere you will actually see it.

**Step 2 — The 30-second reset.**

When you recognise you are off:
1. Name what is happening internally — one word or a short tag ("conflict loop", "too activated right now")
2. Drop your shoulders and breathe out
3. Do one small action: a single sentence of calm response, staying silent, or saying an honest delay phrase

Do not try to resolve the conflict, win the argument, or explain yourself while in this state. The only goal is to not make it worse.

**Step 3 — Use honest delay phrases.**

When you need more time before responding, say something that is actually true. Do not invent a deadline or make up a reason.

Examples of honest delay phrases:
- "I need to check something before I respond to this."
- "I'll come back to this after I look at the details."
- "I want to give this a proper answer — let me get back to you tomorrow."

The test: is the timeline you are giving real? If you are promising to respond by Tuesday but have no intention of doing so by Tuesday, that undermines trust. If the timeline is honest, use the phrase freely.

**Anchoring (optional, only if you do the groundwork):**

Some people use a physical anchor — a small repeated action (touching a knuckle, a specific phrase) that they pair with a calm state over many nights. This only works if you have actually practised pairing it with a genuinely calm moment, at least five or six times. Without that pairing, it is just a gesture.

## Example

You get a message at 10pm that sets off a conflict loop. You know you are going to keep re-reading it. You feel the pull to respond right now.

Instead: shoulders down, breathe out. Say to yourself: "too activated right now". Send nothing. Write in a notepad what you want to say — do not send it. Come back to it in the morning. That is the reset.
