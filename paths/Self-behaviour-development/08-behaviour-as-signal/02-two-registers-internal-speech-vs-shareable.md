# Private thoughts vs what you actually say — keeping them separate

## What this is

Your internal experience can be angry, raw, and extreme. That is fine. That is where it belongs. The mistake is when that raw internal voice leaks into what you write or say to others — especially in writing, which is permanent, and especially anywhere near children or formal settings.

The goal is not to suppress how you feel. It is to translate it. Raw emotion is useful input for understanding what is happening. It is not useful as output, because it lands differently than it was intended, and it can cause harm that outlasts the moment that produced it.

There are two registers:

- **Private register:** candid, unfiltered, for your journal, your therapy session, or your own processing. You can say anything here.
- **Outward register:** calm, factual, purposeful. Not fake warmth — just workable precision. This is what goes into messages, formal exchanges, and anything that could be read by a child, an evaluator, or a court.

## When to use this

- You are about to send a message while angry or hurt
- You are reviewing something you wrote and it feels too hot
- You want to practise translating a strong internal reaction into a usable response
- Something happened and you need to respond formally but feel the raw version surfacing

## How to do it

**Step 1 — Write the raw version privately.**
Get the actual feeling out somewhere that is not a message to anyone. Paper, a private note, or spoken aloud to yourself. Let it be ugly.

**Step 2 — Identify the strategic core.**
Underneath the emotion, what do you actually need to communicate? Strip away the feeling and find the fact or the request.

**Step 3 — Write the outward version.**
Same strategic goal. Different words. No cruelty, no blame language, no exaggeration. Factual and purposeful.

**Step 4 — Review before sending.**
Read the message as if you were someone else — an evaluator, a mediator, or your child reading it in ten years. Does it communicate what matters? Does it contain anything that would look bad to an outside reader? If yes, revise.

## Example

Raw internal version: "She's doing this on purpose to make me look bad and I'm sick of being treated like I'm nothing."

Strategic core: there is a logistics issue that needs to be resolved.

Outward version: "I want to make sure we're aligned on the pick-up time. Can we confirm it is [time] on [date]?"

The feeling is valid. The outward version serves the actual goal.

## The boundary

Feeling something intense does not make it appropriate to send. Calling it "authentic" or "honest" does not justify writing something cruel, especially in any channel where a child could read it. The internal register is yours. The outward register affects other people.
