# Raising your energy — active practices, not just protection

## What this is

Most advice about energy focuses on not losing it — sleep better, eat well, reduce stress. That is important, and it is covered in phase 02. But there is another direction: actively raising your energy level, even when you are tired or low.

This is not about forcing yourself to be high-energy when you are genuinely depleted. It is about having a small set of tools that reliably shift your state upward — practices that take 2 to 15 minutes and actually work.

The practices here are physical. That is not a coincidence. Your mental state follows your physical state more than the other way around. When you feel flat, stuck, or low, the most direct route out is through the body, not through thinking your way to a better mood.

## When to use this

- In the morning when you feel flat and need to start
- Mid-afternoon when energy drops and you have work to do
- Before an important conversation, meeting, or difficult interaction
- When you have been sitting for too long and feel stuck in your head
- When low mood is making everything feel harder than it needs to be

## The practices

### Practice 1 — Morning light (2 minutes, within 30 minutes of waking)

Go outside or stand at a window and get natural light in your eyes for 2 minutes. No sunglasses. This is not about vitamin D — it sets your cortisol rhythm for the day and directly affects energy and mood for the next 12-16 hours.

If you skip this consistently, your energy baseline is lower than it needs to be. It is one of the cheapest things you can do.

---

### Practice 2 — Cold water (30 seconds to 2 minutes)

End your shower with cold water. Start with 30 seconds and work up. It does not need to be an ice bath.

What it does: triggers a noradrenaline release (the chemical responsible for focus and energy), reduces baseline inflammation, and creates a small daily proof to yourself that you can do hard things.

You do not need to enjoy it. You just need to do it.

---

### Practice 3 — Breathing for energy (3-4 minutes)

This is different from slow breathing for calming. This is activating breathing.

**Physiological sigh for reset (30 seconds):**
Double inhale through the nose (inhale, then sniff in a little more air on top), then a long slow exhale through the mouth. Repeat 3 times. This is the fastest known way to reduce stress and shift state.

**Box breathing for focus (4 minutes):**
Inhale 4 counts → hold 4 → exhale 4 → hold 4. Repeat for 4 minutes. Creates calm alertness, not drowsiness.

**Energising breathing (2 minutes):**
Fast rhythmic breathing through the nose — about one breath per second for 20-30 breaths, then a slow exhale and pause. Repeat 2-3 rounds. This raises alertness quickly. (If you feel dizzy, slow down — you are breathing too fast.)

---

### Practice 4 — Movement burst (5-10 minutes)

Any movement that slightly raises your heart rate. Walk briskly for 5 minutes, do 20 push-ups, jump rope for 3 minutes. The specific activity does not matter. The signal to your body does.

After sitting for more than 90 minutes, your brain function measurably drops. Movement resets it.

The minimum viable version: stand up and walk to another room and back. That is enough to restart circulation.

---

### Practice 5 — Work with your natural energy cycles

Your body naturally alternates between periods of higher alertness (about 90 minutes) and lower alertness (about 20 minutes). Most people fight this — they push through the low periods on caffeine or willpower.

A better approach:
- Do your most demanding work during your natural high-energy window (most people: 1-3 hours after waking)
- Take a real break every 90 minutes — even 5 minutes of movement or eyes-closed rest
- Use the afternoon dip (usually 1-3pm) for lower-cognitive tasks: emails, logistics, routine work
- Do not schedule the most important decisions or conversations during your known low-energy windows

Track your energy level hourly for 3 days. You will see your pattern.

---

### Practice 6 — Social energy (often overlooked)

Some interactions reliably raise your energy. Others drain it. Most people know which is which but do not act on this information.

Write down three people whose company genuinely leaves you feeling better. Make contact with at least one of them each week — not for a reason, just for connection. Brief is fine.

Energy is partly social. Isolation during high-stress periods compounds the problem.

## Daily minimum

Pick two of the above and do them every day for two weeks before evaluating. The minimum viable combination: morning light + one movement burst.

## What does not work

- Caffeine as a primary energy tool (it borrows energy from later, then charges interest)
- Forcing yourself to be energetic when you are genuinely depleted (the tools above support recovery, not override it)
- Waiting until you feel like doing the practice (you will not feel like it — do it anyway and see what happens after)
