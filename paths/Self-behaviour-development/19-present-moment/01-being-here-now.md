# Being here now — bringing your attention back to the present

## What this is

At any given moment, your attention can be in one of three places:

- **The past** — replaying what happened, what was said, what went wrong, what you should have done
- **The future** — worrying about what will happen, planning, anticipating, fearing
- **The present** — what is actually happening right now, in this room, in this body, at this moment

Most people under prolonged stress spend the majority of their time in the past or future. This is exhausting in a specific way: the body responds to imagined threats and replayed conflicts almost the same way it responds to real ones. You are stressed about something that is not happening right now.

Being in the present is not a spiritual concept. It is a practical skill: the ability to notice where your attention is and bring it back to what is actually in front of you. It does not make problems disappear. It means you are not carrying yesterday and tomorrow on top of today.

## When to use this

- When you notice you are replaying a conversation that already happened
- When you are worrying about something that has not happened yet and cannot be acted on right now
- When you are physically present but mentally somewhere else (with your child, in a conversation, eating, resting)
- When your mind is racing and nothing on the list is urgent
- Before sleep, when thoughts from the day will not settle

## How to do it

### Practice 1 — The 5-4-3-2-1 ground (2 minutes)

When your mind is somewhere else, this brings it back through the senses.

Name out loud or in your head:
- **5 things you can see** right now
- **4 things you can physically feel** (feet on floor, air on skin, back against chair)
- **3 things you can hear**
- **2 things you can smell** (or would smell if you paid attention)
- **1 thing you can taste**

That is it. Each item forces your attention to the present moment. By the end, you are here.

---

### Practice 2 — The one-breath reset (10 seconds, anywhere)

At any point during the day: one slow breath, and for the duration of that breath, notice only what is physically happening right now. The breath itself. The sensation of air moving. Where your body is in space.

This is the minimum viable present-moment practice. Ten seconds. Does not require stopping what you are doing.

Build the habit by doing it at fixed moments: before opening a message, before starting the car, before eating. Link it to something you already do.

---

### Practice 3 — Single-task blocks (20-45 minutes)

When you are doing something, do only that thing. Not that thing plus worrying about something else.

Pick one task. Set a timer for 20-45 minutes. During that time: only that task. When your mind drifts to something else, notice it and bring it back. Not with frustration — just bring it back.

This is not just productivity advice. The practice of noticing your attention has drifted and returning it is exactly the same skill as noticing you are in the past or future and returning to now.

---

### Practice 4 — The "what is actually happening right now?" check

When you notice you are in a spiral — about the past or the future — ask this one question:

> "What is actually happening right now, in this moment?"

Answer it concretely. Not "my life is a mess" — that is not a present-moment observation. But: "I am sitting on a chair. It is quiet. I am breathing. Nothing is on fire right now."

Often the present moment is okay. It is the past and future that are causing the distress, not what is literally happening right now.

---

### Practice 5 — Fully present for one activity each day

Pick one regular daily activity and do it fully present, every day. Options: one meal (no phone, no screen, just eating), a walk (no podcast, just walking and noticing), the first 10 minutes with your child (nothing else happening), a shower (just the shower).

The activity does not matter. What matters is that for that window, you are fully here. Not somewhere else in your head.

## The pattern to notice

When you find yourself unable to stay present, note where you are going:
- Mostly past → you may be processing something unresolved (connects to CBT tools)
- Mostly future → you may be running anxiety loops (connects to uncertainty tolerance)
- Both → normal during high-stress periods, but worth working with deliberately

## Example

You are having dinner with your child. Physically you are there, but your mind is running through a conversation you had earlier and what you should have said differently.

Notice: "I am not here right now."
Return: look at your child's face. Notice one thing about them. Ask one question and actually listen to the answer.

You have returned to the present. That is the whole practice.
