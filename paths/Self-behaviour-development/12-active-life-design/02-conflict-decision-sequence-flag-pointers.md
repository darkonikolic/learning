# Before engaging in conflict — a quick filter

## What this is

When a conflict situation arises, most people respond on autopilot — either avoiding it entirely, or diving in harder than necessary. Neither is a decision. This is a short filter you run before engaging, so you are choosing how to respond rather than just reacting.

The filter has four questions and four possible responses.

## When to use this

- Before you respond to any message or situation that feels charged
- Before entering a difficult conversation
- At any point mid-conversation when you notice you might be losing ground or draining energy

## How to do it

**Before engaging — ask four questions:**

1. Does this actually matter? Would it affect anything real if you ignored it?
2. Is there a real risk if you do not engage?
3. Is there a specific goal you can actually reach by engaging?
4. What is the most proportionate response?

**Then choose one mode:**

- **Engage fully** — the issue matters, there is real risk, and you have a reachable goal.
- **Minimum only** — say the minimum required. Do not elaborate, do not explain yourself at length.
- **Create distance** — slow down the interaction. Delay, defer, reduce frequency.
- **Exit** — disengage entirely. Not every conflict deserves your time.

**During a long conversation — periodic check:**
At any point where you notice you are working hard but nothing is moving, pause and ask: am I still making progress toward my goal, or am I just draining energy? If the answer is the latter, downgrade your mode.

## Example

You receive a message that provokes you. Before responding: Does this actually matter? Is there real risk? Is there a reachable goal? If the answer to all three is no, the mode is minimum or exit. You write two sentences and stop.
